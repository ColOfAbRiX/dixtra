Bodies

Where each body goes relative to the separator before it: on its line, or below it one level in. The separators are the = of a definition, the => of a lambda or of a case, the then and the else of an if/else, the do of a loop.

A construct with a single body (a definition, a lambda, a loop) takes keep, always-join or always-break. The bodies of a construct with several of them (the branches of an if/else, the cases of a match, the bodies of a try) are one group, never one by one: either every body is on the line of its separator, or every body is below it, and it takes join-mixed, break-mixed, always-join or always-break. A group where some bodies are inline and some others are on a new line is mixed.

Whatever the setting: a body goes below its separator when it cannot be joined on its line, and in a group such a body puts every body of the group below its separator. The settings move bodies only relative to their separators: an else written on its own line stays on its own line, and every case is on its own line. With braces (see braces), a body below its separator is in braces, and a construct whose bodies are on their lines has none.

body.def

Default: keep.

The = of a def.

Before:

def total = xs.sum
def average =
  xs.sum / xs.size

body.def = keep

The body stays where the author wrote it.

def total = xs.sum

def average =
  xs.sum / xs.size

// With settings:
//   max-column = 80
//   body.def = keep

body.def = always-join

The body goes on the line of its separator, even when the author wrote it below.

def total   = xs.sum
def average = xs.sum / xs.size

// With settings:
//   max-column = 80
//   body.def = always-join

body.def = always-break

The body goes below its separator, even when the author wrote it on the line.

def total =
  xs.sum

def average =
  xs.sum / xs.size

// With settings:
//   max-column = 80
//   body.def = always-break
